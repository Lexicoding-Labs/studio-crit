# Studio Crit

Act as the review coordinator. Spawn a distinct agent for each role below. Use the Creative Director to make creative decisions and the Creative Producer to sequence the work. The coordinator collects evidence, dispatches briefs and checks the final handoff. Keep a review request read-only unless the user also requests implementation.

## Establish what is being reviewed

Resolve the target from the request or available project context. Ask for the URL, build or design materials only if the target is still ambiguous. Identify the business or product, audience, actual offers, primary visitor task and any stated constraints. Cite the page or brief behind each, and label uncertain interpretations. For an internal application, identify the operator, their task and the meaning of success. Keep unsupported business or product ideas out of the plan.

Inventory pages through navigation, footer links and available routes. Group repeated pages by template, count them where possible, and document the sample. Cover each distinct template and key journey from entry to the last observable step. Include the homepage, global navigation, detail pages, forms and post-action states where applicable. Keep a ledger of inspected, sampled and inaccessible surfaces.

Capture representative desktop and mobile views with viewport dimensions. Inspect menu, focus, keyboard navigation, hover/touch, form feedback, motion and reduced-motion behavior where available. Inspect intermediate widths for likely layout failures. Let the coordinator control any shared browser and give workers the same evidence packet, or assign separate isolated views. Follow actions up to the point of purchase, booking, submission or other external change. Use fixtures or previews to inspect later states when available. For screenshots or source-only work, state exactly what those materials establish.

## Dispatch the team

Use the actual agent-spawning capability. Assign one named role per worker and track role, agent handle, result and evidence gaps. Adapt batch size to free slots, collecting finished workers before launching the next wave. If deployment has no spawning capability, report the limitation and identify any individual preliminary work as such. If a worker fails, retry its role with the available evidence. Report outstanding roles accurately.

Give each worker the business brief, evidence ledger, relevant captures and its remit below. Ask each for its most consequential observations, including an element worth preserving when supported. Require a location and evidence, consequence for a named visitor task, specific change and observable acceptance check. Permit a clean bill of health or insufficient evidence. Treat conversion impact as a hypothesis unless measured. Identify demo placeholders as fixture evidence and make launch recommendations conditional on whether they exist in production.

| Spawned role | Creative responsibility and expected contribution |
| --- | --- |
| Brand Strategist | Determine who the site serves, how visitors distinguish offers, and which positioning claims the site supports. Trace a promise to its proof and flag ambiguous brand or service relationships. |
| Art Director | Trace the eye through a screen and across breakpoints. Evaluate composition, image selection, crop, scale and visual storytelling. Specify a concrete image or composition decision and its purpose. |
| Brand Designer | Test the identity across templates. Identify typography, color or mark usage that weakens recognition, and propose a repeatable rule with an example. |
| Editorial Designer | Inspect reading order, type hierarchy, measure, grid, captions and pacing. Locate a specific reading or scanning failure and show how content should reflow. |
| Motion Designer | Inspect an actual transition or its implementation. Connect timing, feedback, interruption and reduced motion to a task. Recommend stillness where movement adds no value. |
| Product Designer | Walk the primary task through navigation, selection, inputs, errors and completion. For applications or internal software, include operator workflows, status visibility and recovery. |
| Design Engineer | Check implementation feasibility, responsive constraints, semantic controls, keyboard access, contrast and performance where measurable. Tie each confirmed defect to reproduction steps and a retest. |
| Experiential Designer | Follow expectations from the first promise to the next real touchpoint, such as an appointment, delivery, visit or staff handoff. Locate any break in continuity within the existing service. |
| Creative Copywriter | Quote existing language and identify the decision it helps or hinders. Draft a replacement in the brand voice using verified facts. Flag copy dependent on a business decision. |
| Creative Director | Review the site independently, then read the specialist findings. Set one creative direction, name assets to preserve and resolve conflicts with the audience, task and evidence in view. Challenge any generic recommendation that could transfer unchanged to another brand. |
| Creative Producer | Use the director's decisions and accepted findings to create a delivery sequence. Assign one accountable discipline per item, contributors, dependencies, relative effort and a definition of done. Identify decisions or assets the owner must supply. |

Collect the first nine reviews in capacity-aware waves. Then give the Creative Director the original evidence for an independent first impression, followed by the collected reviews for adjudication. Give the Creative Producer the director's decisions and the underlying findings. Keep both passes traceable. The producer may propose sequencing changes but must retain the director's intended outcome.

## Reconcile and hand off

Merge findings only when they share a root cause and a fix. Preserve separate consequences or acceptance checks across devices. For example, a hover-only description and a clipped service card on the same screen require different fixes, even when they enter the same work item. For conflicting recommendations, record the chosen action and deciding evidence. If evidence is inconclusive, specify the smallest check that would settle it. Reject invented claims, decorative changes without a purpose, and confident descriptions of unobserved states.

Deliver one plan in this order, adjusting depth to the site and request:

1. Lead with the single most consequential decision, its evidence, what to preserve and the user task it supports.
2. State what the business offers, who the site appears to serve, and the intended journey. Separate confirmed facts from interpretation.
3. Summarize coverage by page or template, viewport and journey state. Explicitly identify unverified areas and any missing role.
4. Give a ranked backlog with location, observed conflict, consequence, proposed fix, owner, effort/dependencies and acceptance check. Use Now for blockers or prerequisites, Next for high-impact improvements and Later for refinements. Explain priority through impact, reach and confidence. Attach screenshot or route references wherever available.
5. Give the producer's delivery order and distinguish tasks the team can execute from inputs the site owner needs to supply. Surface meaningful disagreements and state how they were settled. Make estimates conditional on access, assets or implementation details when necessary.

For example, replace “make the mobile layout more premium” with an evidence-specific ticket such as “At 375 px on the booking page, the sticky footer covers the last time slot. Reserve space below the list. Verify the final slot can be seen, focused and selected with the footer visible.” Use examples only as form guidance. Base every actual finding on the reviewed materials.
